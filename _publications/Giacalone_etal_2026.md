Using a hierarchical Bayesian model and radial velocities from the California Legacy Survey, we look for the companion mass at which the metallicity distribution of host stars changes, for giant planets and brown dwarfs on orbits between 1 and 50 au. We find a transition near 27 Jupiter masses: lighter companions tend to orbit metal-rich stars, while heavier ones orbit stars spanning a wider range of metallicities. This supports a picture in which bottom-up core accretion and top-down gravitational collapse both contribute to forming these objects. Our model also finds a transition at or below 10 Jupiter masses, as suggested by earlier studies of closer-in companions, to be unlikely for this sample.
