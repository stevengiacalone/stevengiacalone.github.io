About

I am an NSF Astronomy & Astrophysics Postdoctoral Fellow at Caltech. I study how exoplanets form and evolve in different environments by detecting and characterizing them with ground-based telescopes and large datasets from space missions like TESS. Right now I am comparing the orbits, atmospheres, and host stars of giant planets and brown dwarfs to work out whether the two form the same way.

I earned my PhD in Astrophysics from UC Berkeley in 2023, working with Courtney Dressing to discover planets with TESS and to learn how stellar mass and metallicity shape planetary populations. My thesis focused on hot, terrestrial planets that make favorable JWST targets and on the demographics of planets orbiting hot stars. Along the way I collected data on more than 100 nights at telescopes around the world.

I got my start in research as an undergraduate at the University of Chicago, where I worked with Arieh Königl on simulations of planetary migration and of dust transport in protoplanetary disks by magnetocentrifugal winds. I earned a B.A. in Physics with a specialization in Astrophysics there in 2017.
