About

I am a Postdoctoral Researcher at the NASA Exoplanet Science Institute (NExScI) at Caltech/IPAC. Prior to my current appointment, I was previously an NSF Astronomy & Astrophysics Postdoctoral Fellow in the Caltech Department of Astronomy. I study how planetary systems form and evolve by connecting individual exoplanet discoveries to population-level demographics, using ground-based telescopes and large datasets from space missions like TESS. My current work tackles a fundamental question: do giant planets and brown dwarfs form the same way? I am answering it by measuring their orbits, atmospheres, and host stars to pinpoint where planet-like formation gives way to star-like formation.

I earned my PhD in Astrophysics from UC Berkeley in 2023, working with Courtney Dressing to discover planets with TESS and to learn how stellar mass and metallicity shape planetary populations. My thesis focused on hot, terrestrial planets that make favorable JWST targets and on the demographics of planets orbiting hot stars. Along the way I collected data across more than 100 nights with telescopes around the world.

I got my start in research as an undergraduate at the University of Chicago, where I worked with Arieh Königl on simulations of planetary migration and of dust transport in protoplanetary disks by magnetocentrifugal winds. I earned a B.A. in Physics with a specialization in Astrophysics there in 2017.
